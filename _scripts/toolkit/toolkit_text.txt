Are You Ready

A practical reflection tool for organisations considering collaboration.

=== PAGE 2 ===
WHY THIS TOOLKIT?
Collaboration does not succeed simply because organisations decide to work together. It depends
on conditions that enable organisations to learn, adapt and work effectively together.
Drawing on U-CAN's Practice-Informed Framework for Collaboration, this toolkit helps organisations
reflect on five signals that can influence their readiness to collaborate.
It is not a test of whether organisations are ready or not. It is a tool to surface different perspectives,
identify areas that need attention, and agree on practical actions.
HOW TO USE IT
1 Each organisation completes the statements independently, based on its own perspective
and experience.
2 Rate every statement from 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 5 (Strongly Agree).
3 Come together only after both organisations have completed the assessment.
4 For each signal, compare responses, discuss differences, and agree on an action to strengthen
the collaboration.
HOW TO READ YOUR SCORES
SCORE WHAT IT SUGGESTS
4–5   Strong foundation The condition appears to be well established.
3   Emerging The condition is developing, but may benefit from further
discussion or attention.
1–2   Needs Attention The condition may need to be strengthened before or during
the collaboration.
Important: Differences between organisations are often as useful as the scores themselves.
Where responses differ significantly, use the discussion questions to understand why.

COLLABORATION TOOLKIT · PART 3
Navigating the
Collaboration Journey
A practical reflection tool for navigating collaboration as it evolves

=== PAGE 17 ===
BEFORE YOU BEGIN
This is Part 3 of the U-CAN Collaboration Toolkit, a comprehensive framework to help organisations
understand, navigate and strengthen collaboration.
Parts 1 and 2 helped you assess whether you are ready to collaborate and where collaboration can
create value. Part 3 focuses on what happens once you begin working together: how the
collaboration evolves, where it may need attention, and what you can do to strengthen it.
Collaboration is rarely linear. Organisations may move between stages, revisit earlier stages, or
experience several stages at once. The purpose of this section is therefore not to move through the
stages in sequence, but to identify what the collaboration needs at different points in its journey.
HOW TO USE THIS SECTION
Collaboration is not a linear process. Organisations may move between stages, revisit earlier stages,
or experience several stages at once. Use this section at regular moments to reflect on how the
collaboration is evolving and what it needs next.
The stages are not a fixed sequence, and therefore this part is designed to be used slightly
differently. First, review the four stages and identify which stage, or stages, are most relevant to the
collaboration right now, and then follow the following steps:
1 Each organisation completes the statements independently, based on its own perspective
and experience.
2 Rate every statement from 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 5 (Strongly Agree).
3 Come together only after both organisations have completed the assessment.
4 For each scenario, compare responses, discuss differences, and agree on an action.
Return to these questions as the collaboration evolves. You may move between stages or revisit
an earlier one based on how your collaboration evolves.
=== PAGE 18 ===
THE FOUR STAGES
STAGE WHAT IT IS ABOUT
1. Idea Incubation Exploring ideas together without pressure to formalise.
2. Ways of Working Agreeing how the organisations will work together.
3. Joint Execution Doing the work together and navigating differences.
4. Value Creation Making visible what the collaboration has created and
changed.
Note: These stages are not a checklist or a fixed sequence. The journey is relational, iterative and
shaped by what organisations learn along the way.
HOW TO READ YOUR SCORES
PATTERN WHAT IT SUGGESTS
4–5   Strong The collaboration appears to be working well in this area.
3   Emerging There is some foundation, but this area may need further
attention or clarification.
1–2   Needs attention This area may be creating challenges for the collaboration and
would benefit from focused attention.
Remember: The scores are not a measure of whether the collaboration is “good” or “bad”. They are
a way to surface differences and identify where a conversation or action may be useful.
